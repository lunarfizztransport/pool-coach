What the coach is weighing

The pot sets up the next pot

The best shot is rarely the easiest one. Pick the ball and pocket that leave a makeable angle on your next ball. The coach scores every shot as make chance × quality of the leave.

Cut angle and distance

Make chance drops with cue-to-ball distance times ball-to-pocket distance, and falls fast as the cut gets thinner. A 60° cut from across the table is a low-percentage shot for anyone.

Stun first, spin when needed

Stun is the most predictable shot. On a cut, a stunned cue ball leaves at 90° to the object ball's path. Follow bends it forward (about 30° off its line on a half-ball hit). Draw bends it back, and it weakens over distance.

A little angle is good

Dead straight leaves only forward or back. A slight angle lets you send the cue ball anywhere along the tangent line, so aim to leave yourself 10–30° cuts.

Speed beats spin

Most position errors are speed errors. Soft and medium shots are more accurate. Hard shots cost make percentage and scatter the cue ball.

When to play safe

If your best pot is under about 45% and a miss would leave your opponent open, play a safety. Hit your own ball first, get a ball to a rail, and leave the cue ball where they have no clear shot.

Solve problems early

Balls frozen together or stuck on a rail get harder as the table empties. Break clusters while you still have other balls to fall back on.

9-ball is a position game

You must hit the lowest ball first, so you never choose the ball. You choose the pocket, spin and speed that get you on the next ball in order. Making the 9 on any legal shot wins, and a missed shot on a tough ball is often better played as a safety.

Kicking with the mirror system

When your ball is blocked, picture the table flipped across a rail and aim at the ball's mirror image. Real cushions take speed off the bounce, so adjust from there: Kicks mode shows the mirror aim and the real aim in diamonds, so you learn the correction.

The push out

On the shot right after the break you may push: roll the cue ball anywhere, with no ball or rail needed. Your opponent then chooses who shoots. Push to a shot that's close to a coin flip, so their choice doesn't help them.

Watch the scratch lines

Before shooting, trace where the cue ball goes after contact. If that line runs at a pocket, change spin or speed. The coach flags spin and speed combos that scratch.
